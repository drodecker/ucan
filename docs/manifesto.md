# UCAN Manifesto

## Reclaiming the Internet's Namespace

We, the builders, operators, and users of the Internet, believe that the authority to create and delegate digital namespaces should belong to the communities that build and operate networks—not to any single organization.

The Internet flourished because of open standards, interoperable protocols, and decentralized operation. Yet one of its most fundamental resources—the global namespace—remains governed through a centralized hierarchy.

Today, the creation of new top-level namespaces ultimately depends upon a single administrative path, which delegates responsibility to registries, registrars, and other intermediaries. This model has provided consistency and stability, but it also creates barriers to experimentation and concentrates authority over a resource essential to communication, identity, commerce, and public life.

UCAN exists to explore a different path.

UCAN—the Universal Cooperative Addressing Network—is an open protocol proposal for decentralized namespace delegation. Rather than replacing the Domain Name System, UCAN seeks to build upon it by allowing namespace authority to be established and extended through verifiable delegation.

## Our principles

### Open participation

Anyone who can prove authority over an existing namespace should be able to extend that authority into new namespaces without depending upon a single centralized approval process.

### Cooperative governance

Internet naming should evolve through open standards, transparent processes, and broad participation rather than unilateral control.

### Incremental adoption

UCAN should complement existing Internet infrastructure, not require its abandonment. DNS operators, registries, registrars, resolvers, hosting providers, and network operators may all participate.

### Cryptographic trust

Authority should be independently verifiable. Delegations should be transparent, auditable, time-bounded where appropriate, and revocable through explicit mechanisms.

### Protocol neutrality

UCAN defines a framework for delegation and validation. It is not inherently a blockchain, cryptocurrency, company, or business model.

### Universal scope

Future networks may be sovereign, private, disconnected, delay-tolerant, or interplanetary. They will require local delegation authority while retaining the ability to interoperate with other naming systems.

## Why this matters

The Internet's namespace is no longer merely a technical convenience. It is part of the foundation of digital identity, commerce, communication, and trust.

A naming architecture designed for one administrative root and one planet may not be sufficient for every future network.

UCAN seeks to develop a model in which independent communities can establish trusted namespace authority while preserving interoperability wherever cooperation is possible.

## Our goal

UCAN is not a product.

It is not a company.

It is not a token.

It is an open protocol proposal intended to foster discussion, experimentation, implementation, and eventual standardization.

We invite operators, registries, registrars, protocol designers, researchers, developers, standards participants, and users to help test and improve the idea.

The Internet succeeded because its protocols were open.

Its future namespace systems should be open as well.
